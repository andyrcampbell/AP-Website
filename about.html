About

Alternative Photographic began as a record of attention — places and moments encountered over decades of travel with a camera. It has become a programme of strictly limited fine art editions for collectors.

The Photographer

Andy Campbell

Andy Campbell's engagement with photography began at Bishop Wordsworth's Grammar School, in the shadow of Salisbury Cathedral in England, in the early 1970s. His first job was at Chris Nichol's Cameras, by the Poultry Cross in Salisbury — where he purchased his first camera: a Zenith B fitted with a Helios 58mm f/2 lens, a piece of optical engineering that has since acquired its own place in photographic history. During these early years Andy spent many days in the darkroom learning the art of black and white photography before transitioning to color. The relationship between maker, tool, and subject was established early.

For more than three decades Andy shot exclusively on Kodak Ektachrome transparency film. Slide film is an unforgiving medium: exposure must be correct at the moment of capture. There is no latitude to recover a misfired frame in post-production, no margin to be reclaimed later. That discipline — pre-visualise, meter carefully, expose correctly, let the light do the work — remains the foundation of the approach.

His first digital camera arrived in 2005. The photographs on this site are made with Canon digital cameras and lenses. The change in capture medium altered the instrument; it did not alter the commitment to what a well-made photograph ought to look like. The analogue archive — spanning those three decades of Ektachrome — is currently being restored, and will be presented on this site under Legacy Work as it becomes available.

The Work

Photography, at its best, is not documentation. It is argument — a claim about what matters, made at a particular moment in a particular place. The images in this catalogue are the residue of that argument: landscapes pressed against their own extremity, urban spaces caught in the brief interval between one version of themselves and the next, figures encountered in the peripheral vision of travel.

The work spans several decades and four continents. It has no unifying style — there is no filter, no signature palette, no house look imposed from outside the image. What holds it together is a commitment to stillness and to the quality of light that only comes when you have waited long enough.

The subjects range from the Grand Canal at first light to the mist-blanked surface of Lexington Reservoir, at the gateway to the Santa Cruz Mountains, in January. What they share is the sense that the photograph was made at the one moment when the world briefly held a particular arrangement of itself — and that missing that moment would have been a loss that could not be recovered.

Edition Discipline

On Editions

Each work in the Alternative Photographic catalogue is produced in a strictly limited edition of 50 numbered impressions, pooled across all three size formats — Entry, Standard, and Exhibition — so that 50 impressions in total exist across all formats combined. In addition, the Studio reserves the right to produce up to two artist's proofs at each format for archival purposes — a maximum of six per work in total. Artist's proofs are not offered for sale. The total authorised maximum of all impressions from a single master is therefore up to 56.

Once the numbered edition is complete it is permanently closed: no further numbered impressions of any kind will be produced or offered. The Studio's digital master files are retained — they are not destroyed or cancelled. The edition limit is a binding commitment by the Studio, not a physical impossibility created by destruction of the master. For the numbered limited edition, there are no reprints, no late additions, and no expanded runs. Each numbered impression is one of at most 50, signed by the artist and accompanied by a Certificate of Authenticity. From the same master, the Studio also offers an unlimited open-edition print at $25 — unsigned, unnumbered, and without a certificate. These are separate products with different structures, prices, and documentation.

This is not a marketing position. It is a material commitment to the collectors who hold these works. The value of an edition lies in part in the certainty that it will not be diluted. When the last print in a run is placed, that run is sealed.

Each print is numbered within the edition — not sequentially in order of production, but in order of placement — so that a collector knows exactly where their print sits within the complete run.

Craft

Materials & Process

All prints in the Alternative Photographic catalogue are produced to archival standards. The specification is consistent across the entire edition — no variation between early and late prints in a run.

Substrate

All editions are printed on archival fine art media using pigment inks. The specific paper stock, ink set, and printer for each size tier of each work are confirmed to each collector in writing at the time of acceptance. Materials for a given tier of a given work are consistent across all impressions produced in that tier’s print run.

Inks

Dye-based inks are not used at any stage of production.

Finishing

Prints are available unmounted, float-mounted, or framed. Frame specifications are discussed and confirmed with each collector at the point of enquiry. Framing is handled by specialist conservators.

Documentation

Certificate of Authenticity

Every numbered limited-edition print placed with a collector is accompanied by a Certificate of Authenticity. Open-edition prints carry no certificate. The certificate records the title of the work, the edition number, the edition size, the date of production, the substrate and ink specification, the studio's commitment to the permanent closure of the edition on completion, and the disposition of the digital master — in the case of Alternative Photographic editions, the master files are retained by the Studio and will not be destroyed or cancelled. The certificate is signed and embossed.

The certificate travels with the work. In the event of subsequent sale or transfer to a new collection, the certificate transfers with it — this is not a document for the first buyer. It is a record of provenance that accompanies the print for the duration of its existence.

On finalisation of each sale, the studio intends to register a corresponding NFT on the Hedera network carrying the title, edition number, and photographer's name — a digital record of title designed to travel with the work through any subsequent change of ownership.

Fulfilment

Shipping & Delivery

Fine art prints require care that standard courier services cannot provide. All prints are packed and shipped by specialists in fine art transport.

Packing

Each print is interleaved with acid-free tissue and packed in a custom-sized rigid case. Framed works are crated individually.

Insurance

All prints are fully insured for their agreed value during transit. Insurance documentation is provided with the shipping confirmation.

Timelines

Production and fulfilment typically takes six to eight weeks from confirmed placement. This is the time required to produce a print to the standard we maintain. We do not rush production.
